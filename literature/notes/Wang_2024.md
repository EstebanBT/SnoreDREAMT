# Wang et al. (2024) — DREAMT: wearable + PSG dataset from a sleep-disorder clinic

## TL;DR
Introduces DREAMT (Dataset for Real-time sleep stage EstimAtion using Multisensor wearable Technology): 100 patients of the Duke Sleep Disorders Center wore an Empatica E4 wristband during a single clinical overnight PSG, with technician-scored 30-s sleep stages and retrospectively added apnea/hypopnea event labels. The modelling part (sleep vs wake from E4 only) shows that a mixed-effects boosted tree (GPBoost, random effect = apnea severity or obesity) plus a shallow LSTM post-processor beats plain LightGBM (best F1 0.823 ± 0.019, AUROC 0.926 ± 0.016, kappa 0.694 ± 0.025). **The paper describes the PSG only briefly (one paragraph in Appendix A.2) and says the PSG data are "available upon reasonable request"; the 100 Hz PSG files our project uses are not described in this paper.**

## Research question & hypotheses
- Existing wearable sleep-tracking algorithms are trained on healthy, young, mostly female samples and generalise poorly to people with sleep disorders; open datasets with wearable + PSG labels in clinical populations are lacking (Intro 1.1–1.2, Supp. Tables 1–2).
- Aims: (1) release such a dataset; (2) show that modelling group-wise random effects (obesity, apnea severity) and temporal structure (LSTM post-processing) improves sleep/wake detection in this population (Sections 2.6–2.7).

## Sample & design

### Recruitment and cohort (Section 2.2, Table 1, Appendix A.2–A.3)
- N = 100 unique participants, recruited from the Duke Sleep Disorder Lab between May 2022 and September 2022. Patients were there for a clinical sleep study as part of usual care; the clinician asked for willingness to join after check-in. "There were no selection criteria for the potential participants since we were aiming for people with sleep apnea" (A.2). At most 4 participants per night (device limit). Participants who stopped participation were removed (2.4).
- IRB #Pro00108961 (Duke, DUHS IRB); written informed consent with explicit permission to share de-identified data.
- Sex: 45 male, 55 female. Age range 21–87 y, mean 56.2 ± 16.6 y (text) / 56 ± 17 (Table 1).

- Obesity defined as BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² (68 participants obese or severely obese). Severe OSA defined in text as AHI > 30 /h: 23 participants, 17 of them obese. "AHI < 5/h is healthy" (2.2). Distribution of apnea severity and obesity categories only in Supp. Fig. 1 (image, not extracted).
- Self-/clinically reported sleep disorders or symptoms (2.2): snoring n = 40, excessive daytime sleepiness n = 34, sleep apnea n = 56, obstructive sleep apnea n = 33, restless sleep (e.g., RLS) n = 23, difficulty breathing (e.g., gasping) n = 22. These categories overlap. The paper does not say whether these come from self-report, referral diagnosis, or the PSG.
- "There are 26 participants who do not have sleep apnea", but they have other conditions (depression, anxiety, hypertension, asthma) (4.1 Limitations). All 100 are expected to have some sleep abnormality. Separately, 22 subjects with AHI < 5 **and** < 20% artefact epochs were used for a sub-analysis (A.4).
- Most common comorbidities/diagnoses are included per participant in the release (`participant info.csv`), but not full medical history (2.2, 2.4).

### Protocol (Section 2.2, Fig. 1, Appendix A.2)
- Check-in ~9 PM; E4 put on the **left wrist** right after consent; PSG started "soon after". Recording continued overnight and "ended when the participant awakened around 6 AM the next morning naturally"; E4 deactivated at awakening; both removed at the same time.
- Clinic protocol: requires 6 h of sleep data; "all patients follow a 10PM - 6AM sleep schedule" (A.2); "every participant started their sleep in the clinic around 10 PM, woke up around 6 AM" (4.1). Note the tension between a fixed 10 PM–6 AM schedule and "awakened … naturally".
- Caffeine abstention requested after 12 PM on the recording day; not monitored (A.2).
- Hotel-like single rooms on the same floor of the sleep lab.
- The clinic protocol "is designed to detect and monitor patients' apnea events during sleep" (A.2), i.e., diagnostic studies. Whether any nights were split-night or CPAP titration studies is **not stated**.

### PSG recording (Appendix A.2 — the only description in the paper)
- System: "Nihon Khoden [sic, = Nihon Kohden] Polysmith (version 11) Data Management System (DMS)". "A total of sixteen channels were recorded."
- EEG: 6 channels, Grass disc electrodes, standard 10/20 placement: **C4-M1, F4-M1, O2-M1, Fp1-O2, T3-Cz, Cz-T4** (as printed; Fp1-O2 is an unusual derivation and may be a typo).
- Airflow: "measured using an oronasal thermal sensor and a nasal air pressure transducer microphone for snoring" (sentence as printed; ambiguous whether snoring is a separate microphone or derived from the nasal pressure transducer — see Limitations).
- Respiratory effort: thoracic and abdominal inductance plethysmography (RIP).
- Chin EMG (mentalis); bilateral tibialis anterior leg EMG (3M adhesive red dot electrodes); EOG and ECG recorded (montage not specified).
- SpO2: finger probe into the Polysmith DMS (Table 1 labels it "SaO2").
- "The amplifiers and other hardware vary in age from 2006-2022."
- **Not reported**: native sampling rate(s), hardware/software filters, impedances, EOG/ECG montage, ground/reference details beyond the derivations above, signal calibration, channel names in the released files, any resampling to 100 Hz.

### Scoring and annotation (Sections 2.2, 3.1, 4; Appendix B.2)
- Sleep stages W, R, N1, N2, N3, and "Missing" (no stage labelled), annotated by "the Sleep Disorder Lab's technicians" in 30-s epochs, then time-aligned with E4 timestamps (2.2). Acknowledgments credit the Duke Sleep Disorders Center for the annotations.
- Scoring manual (e.g., AASM version), number of scorers per record, inter-scorer reliability, and arousal-scoring rules are **not reported**.
- Apnea events: "we retrospectively recorded labels for sleep apnea events (including central apnea, hypopnea, and obstructive apnea)" (2.2); elsewhere "apnea event labels by expert sleep technicians" (4). Hypopnea criterion (3% vs 4% desaturation / arousal), apnea definitions, event timing resolution and how "retrospectively" was done are **not reported**. Snoring annotations are **not mentioned**.
- AHI, OAHI, arousal index, mean SaO2 and BMI were clinical measurements from the study night (2.2, Table 1).
- Missing labels (3.1): labelled missing when PSG was missing or not annotated. Two participants had their **PSG re-set-up during the night → 15 min of consecutive missing labels each**; four other participants had one missing epoch each.
- Epoch totals across all 100: 8,636 REM, 52,915 NREM (N1–N3 combined), 20,334 Wake (2.3). Per participant: 12 ± 6% REM, 64 ± 14% NREM, 25 ± 17% Wake. (Totals ≈ 81,885 epochs ≈ 682 h, ~6.8 h per participant — my arithmetic.) Stage-wise N1/N2/N3 counts not given.

### Wearable (Sections 2.1, 2.3, Appendix B.2)
- Empatica E4: BVP 64 Hz, ACC (X/Y/Z) 32 Hz, EDA 4 Hz, TEMP 4 Hz, HR 1 Hz (derived from BVP via IBI, proprietary algorithm; HR carried forward when IBI unavailable), IBI irregular.
- Released E4 files: one CSV per participant with columns TIMESTAMP, BVP, IBI, EDA, TEMP, ACC X, ACC Y, ACC Z, HR, Sleep Stage; all upsampled to 64 Hz by repeating values (last observation carried forward, "no imputation"). Folder overview lists `E4 aggregate/`, `features df/`, `participant info.csv` (underscores likely lost in conversion).
- E4 and stage labels "joined together by the datetime of the data point"; real times replaced by time since first sample (privacy). Synchronisation accuracy between E4 and PSG clocks is **not reported**.
- Hosting: PhysioNet, credentialed access, PhysioNet Credentialed Health Data License 1.5.0 (B.1). At time of writing the dataset was "under review" at PhysioNet.

## Methods (replication-level detail)
Only relevant to the wearable modelling, summarised for completeness.

### E4 preprocessing (Section 2.5)
| Signal | Processing |
|---|---|
| All | Upsampled to 64 Hz, LOCF |
| ACC | 5th-order Butterworth band-pass 3–11 Hz (Oura parameters, Altini & Kinnunen 2021); trimmed mean (10% each side), max, IQR per axis per 30-s window of absolute filtered signal; MAD of raw ACC relative to vector magnitude |
| TEMP | Winsorised to 31–40 °C (affected 10% of values); mean, min, max, SD |
| BVP | Chebyshev type II band-pass 0.5–20 Hz (printed "0.520 Hz"); NeuroKit2 HRV: rMSSD, SDNN, pNN50, LF (0.05–0.15 Hz), HF (0.15–0.4 Hz), peak freqs, total/normalised power, breathing rate |
| EDA | Detrend per 5-s segment (least-squares line), Butterworth low-pass (order/cutoff not given), NeuroKit2 SCL/SCR decomposition; SCR peak height/amplitude, rise and recovery time; epoch mean and max |

- Signal quality (after Moscato et al. 2022): a 30-s segment is an artefact if Activity Index > 0.4125, raw BVP outside −500 to 500, or BVP SNR < 10 dB (BVP band 0.5–20 Hz).
- Feature engineering: per-epoch features, then per-participant temporal Gaussian smoothing (σ not reported), temporal derivative of smoothed feature, moving-window variance (window not reported).

### Modelling (Sections 2.6–2.7, 3.1)
- QC: participants with artefacts in > 20% of epochs removed → **80 participants** retained (E4-based QC, not PSG-based).
- Task: sleep vs wake per 30-s epoch; wake = positive class. Missing-label epochs dropped; for the LSTM, epochs either side of a missing epoch treated as adjacent.
- Models: LightGBM baseline; GPBoost (Sigrist 2020) with random effect = obesity or apnea severity; each ± single-layer LSTM post-processing taking predicted probabilities plus the two top SHAP features (ACC INDEX, HRV Higuchi fractal dimension), chosen from a separate 56/8/16 train/val/test split.
- 5-fold participant-level CV: 16 test, 56 train, 8 validation per fold. HyperOpt tuning; validation set for early stopping; SMOTE on training set only.
- LSTM hidden size, sequence length, training details not reported. Hardware: RTX 4090, AMD Ryzen 9 7900X (A.4).

## Key results
- Table 2 (80 participants, 5-fold CV, mean ± SD across folds):
  - LightGBM: F1 0.777 ± 0.009, AUROC 0.895 ± 0.007, AUPRC 0.885 ± 0.008, Acc 0.816 ± 0.008, κ 0.605 ± 0.024.
  - GPBoost + obesity: F1 0.785, AUROC 0.902, κ 0.622. GPBoost + apnea severity: F1 0.782, AUROC 0.898, κ 0.623.
  - LightGBM + LSTM: F1 0.805, AUROC 0.915, κ 0.649.
  - GPBoost + obesity + LSTM: F1 0.822 ± 0.019, AUROC 0.926 ± 0.011, AUPRC 0.914, Acc 0.853, κ 0.683 ± 0.028.
  - **Best: GPBoost + apnea severity + LSTM**: F1 0.823 ± 0.019, AUROC 0.926 ± 0.016, AUPRC 0.915 ± 0.020, Acc 0.857 ± 0.016, κ 0.694 ± 0.025 (abstract says κ 0.695 ± 0.025).
- No significance tests between models; improvements from random effects are small relative to fold SD.
- No-apnea subgroup (n = 22, AHI < 5 and < 20% artefact epochs; Supp. Table 3): LSTM still helps (best κ 0.543 ± 0.048) but random effects no longer help — interpreted as expected (4, A.4).
- LSTM corrects false positives/negatives by learning that wake clusters at the start and end of the sleep opportunity (Fig. 3).

## Limitations
- Stated by authors:
  - Only two random effects (obesity, apnea severity) examined; other variables (mental health, cardiovascular, non-apnea sleep disorders) not explored (4.1).
  - Features are domain-driven, not learned (4.1).
  - Few participants with normal sleep; all 100 expected to have a sleep disorder; 26 without sleep apnea still have conditions affecting sleep (4.1).
  - Clinic setting and fixed ~10 PM–6 AM schedule may not match participants' habitual sleep (4.1).
  - Caffeine abstention not monitored (A.2).
  - Amplifiers/hardware vary in age from 2006–2022 (A.2).
  - Timestamps time-shifted; exact start time withheld; full medical history withheld (2.4).
- Not stated by authors:
  - **PSG documentation is minimal**: no sampling rate, filters, impedance, scoring manual, hypopnea definition, number of scorers or reliability. The paper does not describe the released PSG files at all (says PSG is available "upon reasonable request"), so it cannot be the source for channel names, units or the 100 Hz sampling in the files we use — that must come from the PhysioNet DREAMT documentation/version notes.
  - Apnea/hypopnea labels were made "retrospectively"; whether by the same technicians, with the same rules, and at what time resolution is unknown. No snoring annotations described.
  - The snoring sensor sentence is ambiguous ("nasal air pressure transducer microphone for snoring"): snoring may be a separate microphone or derived from the nasal-pressure signal. At 100 Hz (Nyquist 50 Hz), a snore channel cannot hold the acoustic waveform (snoring energy extends well above 50 Hz), so whatever the SNORE channel in the 100 Hz files contains is probably an envelope/derived or aliased signal — needs checking.
  - Snoring (n = 40), apnea (n = 56) and other diagnoses are reported counts with unclear origin (self-report vs diagnosis); they overlap.
  - Medications (hypnotics, antidepressants, stimulants), CPAP use, and split-night status not reported — all can alter SO/spindle activity.
  - Internal inconsistencies: Table 1 total BMI 33.7 does not equal the n-weighted group mean (≈ 32.6; all other Table 1 totals do match their weighted means); older-adult age SD "74 ± 1" is implausible for a 65–87 range; severe OSA as AHI "> 30" (conventional cut-off is ≥ 30); "Nihon Khoden" misspelling; abstract κ 0.695 vs table 0.694; Section 2.3 calls the label "REM Sleep, Non-REM Sleep, Wake" while 2.2/B.2 describe W/R/N1/N2/N3.
  - Recording start vs lights-off is not marked in the paper; the 25 ± 17% wake per participant includes pre-sleep/post-wake periods and is not WASO.
  - The E4-based QC (80 retained) says nothing about PSG/EEG quality.

## Relevance to this project
DREAMT is our data source, so this paper is the citation for the cohort and acquisition, but it is thin on exactly what we need. Practical consequences:

- **What to cite from here**: Duke Sleep Disorders Center, May–Sep 2022, N = 100 (45 M / 55 F), age 21–87 (56.2 ± 16.6), BMI 33.7 ± 8.6, AHI 22.1 ± 28.7/h, OAHI 19.4 ± 27.5/h, arousal index 37.7 ± 25.7/h; Nihon Kohden Polysmith v11; EEG on Grass disc electrodes incl. C4-M1 and F4-M1; oronasal thermistor + nasal pressure; thoracic/abdominal RIP; technician-scored 30-s stages; retrospectively annotated central/obstructive apneas and hypopneas. Take channel names (PTAF, FLOW, THORAX, ABDOMEN, SNORE), the 100 Hz sampling, label columns and any extra stage codes from the PhysioNet DREAMT documentation, not from this paper.
- **SO/spindle detection**: C4-M1 and F4-M1 are standard AASM derivations, which suits SO (frontal) and spindle (central) detection. 100 Hz sampling is enough for SOs and spindles, but we don't know the anti-alias/hardware filters, and amplifiers range from 2006 to 2022, so we should check spectra and amplitude scaling per recording and normalise detection thresholds within participants (e.g., percentile-based, as in Schreiner et al. 2023/Bullón Tarrasó et al. 2026), not use absolute µV cut-offs.
- **Respiration phase**: nasal pressure (PTAF) tracks flow best but flattens with mouth breathing and is disturbed by snoring vibration and flow limitation, which are the very conditions we are comparing. The thermistor (FLOW) is slower and phase-lagged, and the RIP belts measure effort, which keeps going during obstructive events. Plan to compare phase estimates across PTAF, FLOW and THORAX/ABDOMEN, and show that any "lost coupling" during snoring is not just a sensor-phase artefact.
- **Snoring definition**: self-reported snoring (n = 40) cannot define snoring epochs. We need an objective, epoch- or breath-level snore detector from the SNORE channel (and/or high-frequency PTAF content). First establish what the SNORE channel is at 100 Hz (envelope vs raw), because the paper doesn't say.
- **Major confounds**: this is a clinical cohort with high AHI and a high arousal index. Snoring co-occurs with hypopneas, flow limitation, arousals and obesity. Snoring vs non-snoring contrasts should (i) exclude windows around annotated apneas/hypopneas (and probably arousals, though arousal events may not be in the files), (ii) be within-participant where possible, and (iii) include age (21–87; SO/spindle density falls with age), AHI/OAHI and BMI as covariates. The hypopnea definition is unknown, so events below that threshold (RERAs, flow limitation) will be unlabelled and will sit inside "clean" snoring windows.
- **Data-quality checks to build in**: 2 participants had a PSG re-set-up with 15 min of missing labels, and 4 had single missing epochs. Check for discontinuities and channel changes at those points. Verify the stage-label/PSG alignment (labels were aligned by datetime to the E4 data; the alignment to the 100 Hz PSG files should be checked independently). Don't reuse the paper's E4-artefact exclusion (80/100) as a PSG exclusion criterion.
- **Unknowns to note in our Methods**: medications, CPAP/split-night status, scoring manual and hypopnea rule. Either state them as limitations or try to get them from the PhysioNet metadata or participant_info.
